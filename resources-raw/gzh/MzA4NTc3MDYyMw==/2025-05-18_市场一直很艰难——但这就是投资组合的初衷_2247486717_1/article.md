3\. Easy Rider投资组合  
  - 这是一个长期投资组合，包含全球多元化的资产配置（股票、债券、黄金、大宗商品，可选比特币），每年重新平衡一次。

  - 这是一个动态资产配置策略，每月调整投资于表现最佳的全球资产（股票、债券、黄金、大宗商品），并在市场无趋势时转向安全资产。  
  - 目标是实现类似股市的回报，同时减少损失。

  - 专注于美国股市的行业轮动，持有表现最佳的行业，并在无上涨趋势时转向债券。  
  - 设计目的是抓住市场周期，同时避免大幅下跌，每月仅需少量交易。

# The Market’s Been Tough – But That’s What The Finimize Portfolios Were Built For

###   

### Idea

Markets haven’t made it easy this year. Leading stocks have become losing stocks, the S&P 500 has given up its edge, and sentiment has proved once again that it can turn on a dime. But through the chaos and the red down arrows, the Finimize model portfolios have quietly delivered. Each one of them is ahead of the S&P 500 so far this year. (The Momentum Rider Portfolio, for one, is nearly 10 percentage points out in front.) That’s not luck: that’s the power of a rules-based strategy that adapts to the market – not to your gut instincts.

### Thesis

-   The Easy Rider Portfolio is a strategic, long-term investing mix. It holds a fixed, globally diversified blend of stocks, bonds, gold, and other commodities (plus optional bitcoin), and rebalances once a year. It’s designed to help long-term investors stay on track across a range of economic scenarios – with fewer bumps in the road than you’d get with a stock-heavy portfolio.
-   The Momentum Rider Portfolio is a dynamic asset allocation strategy. It pivots into the best-performing global assets each month – stocks, bonds, gold, and other commodities – and moves to safety when nothing’s trending. It’s a rules-based approach that adapts automatically to market trends, aiming for stock-like returns with smaller losses along the way.
-   The Sector Momentum Edge rotates across US stock sectors, staying with the best performers and stepping into bonds when no sector is in an uptrend. It’s designed to ride market cycles and avoid big drawdowns, with just a few trades each month.

### Key risks

No strategy works all the time. Each of the Finimize portfolios is designed to tilt the odds in your favor – but they all come with trade-offs you’ll need to bear in mind:

They can struggle at turning points. Momentum strategies often get whipsawed when the market’s leaders quickly flip. Plus, diversified portfolios can still take hits when several things drop together. And none of these strategies will predict the market “top” – or “bottom” – perfectly.

They won’t always beat the market – especially not when US stocks are flying.These strategies swap a bit of upside for more consistency and better risk management.

They take discipline – and a bit of work. Easy Rider is low maintenance (you rebalance just once a year), but Momentum Rider and Sector Momentum Edge both need a quick monthly check-in. That’s not day trading, but it’s not fully passive either.

They go against the crowd. A rules-based approach means you’ll sometimes hold assets no one’s talking about – and sit out rallies in the ones on everybody’s lips. That can feel uncomfortable, but it’s exactly what makes these strategies work over time.

## PART 1: The Finimize Easy Rider Portfolio

Look only at performance over the past few years, and you might be tempted to go all-in on US stocks. But with a ten-year horizon, the odds of landing some disappointing returns or hitting a really rough patch are high. The Easy Rider Model Portfolio offers a humbler approach: it mixes stocks with bonds, gold, and other commodities, and even lets you sprinkle in some bitcoin if that’s your thing. The goal is to help you do more than just chase returns – shrinking your risk and boosting your chances of meeting your financial goals.

### Who’s this strategy for (and who is it not for)?

The Easy Rider Portfolio is designed for investors who care more about reaching their long-term goals than shooting the lights out. I’m talking about the folks who don’t want to bet everything on the notion that the next decade will look like the last one – and who’d rather own a balanced mix that can handle a range of scenarios. It’s also for people who prefer a hands-off, low-maintenance approach, who are happy to trade a bit of upside for fewer sleepless nights.

In other words, it’s not for the rose-colored-glasses crowd, or the performance chasers, or the players who judge a portfolio’s success by how it stacks up to the S&P 500 every quarter. This Easy Rider’s broader diversification means it’ll lag when US stocks are flying – and that’s entirely by design.

### What are the rules?

This is a long-term asset allocation strategy with a fixed mix that gets rebalanced once a year.

It holds a globally diversified set of assets: 60% in stocks (split across the US, other developed markets, and emerging markets), 20% in government bonds (US and international), 10% in commodities, 7.5% in gold, and 2.5% in bitcoin. Or, if you’d rather not dabble in bitcoin, you could simply allocate 10% to gold instead.

### How has it performed historically?

From January 1997 through April of this year, the Easy Rider (excluding bitcoin, which didn’t exist in those earlier years) returned an average of 7.5% annually. That’s just a tad sweeter than the 7.3% that global stocks delivered, and with far less drama along the way. The portfolio made money in nearly two-thirds of all those months, and its biggest annual drops were a lot milder than what you’d see with stocks alone.

During the global crisis in 2008, the portfolio slipped just 35%, while international stocks tumbled by 55%. It proved far more resilient during the 2020 Covid crash and 2022’s inflation\-fueled selloff, too. Those smaller drawdowns and swifter recoveries made it a lot easier to stay invested – and a lot less likely that you’d panic and sell at the worst possible moment.

### Why does it work?

Short answer: because it’s built for better balance.

The Easy Rider hangs onto a set of assets that behave differently in different economic environments. Stocks thrive when growth is strong and inflation is low. Bonds perk up when growth disappoints, inflation falls, or investors panic. Commodities shine when growth and inflation are both strong. And gold steps up when the pace of growth slows, but inflation remains quick. Oh, and bitcoin can be a real advantage when unintended consequences pop up from major central bank or government spending decisions.

I gave the portfolio its cool-hand name for a reason: its mix has helped steady the ride over time – reducing volatility, capping losses, and helping investors stay calm, even when conditions change.

### What are the risks?

The Easy Rider isn’t bulletproof. With 60% in stocks, it can still take a hit – it just won’t take it as hard as an all-stocks portfolio would. And, if US stocks outperform (as they’re known to do), this portfolio won’t keep pace with them.

That’s the trade-off: more balance, less concentration, and a higher chance of staying invested when things get rough – but lower highs when investors are euphoric.

### How has it performed since its inception?

Solidly. This portfolio is up 3% since I built it last November – overshadowing global stocks, with their 0.7% gain, and beating US stocks, with their 2% loss.

Look at its year-to-date performance and it’s even prettier: up 2.8%, while US stocks have dropped 5%. That’s nearly eight percentage points of outperformance – in just five months.

US government bonds have added 3% while international government bonds have kicked in 7%. Diversifying across regions has helped too: developed market stocks have risen 10%, and emerging market stocks 3%.

### PART 2: The Finimize Momentum Rider Portfolio

Past winners aren’t always future winners (the market loves to remind us of this). Commodities and emerging markets stocksled the pack before 2008, then mostly straggled. US stocks took over the dominant role – but they’ve given it up so far this year. And, yeah, you can set out to diversify your investing mix, but that comes with baggage too. It means you’re always holding some of the market’s slowpokes, and it won’t shield you from every downturn. That’s why the Momentum Rider Portfolio does things differently. It doesn’t make bold predictions… it adapts. By backing the top-performing assets each month and moving to bonds when nothing’s working, it aims to deliver stock-like returns across a wider range of scenarios – with fewer nasty drawdowns along the way.

### Who’s this strategy for (and who is it not for)?

The Momentum Rider is for investors who don’t want to bet it all on one winner – like US stocks – but who don’t want to spread themselves too thin either. It’s a concentrated, high-conviction portfolio that adapts to what’s actually working at the time, with built-in risk management that cuts its losses when trendsturn. The idea is to remain active with a monthly check-in, but trade just a handful of times a year.

It’s not for investors who expect to chase every rally and beat the S&P 500 every single year. Momentum can be slow to catch up in choppy or fast-moving markets – and it takes patience to stick with it when it falls out of sync.

### What are the rules?

The Momentum Rider draws from the same ETF lineup as the Easy Rider, so you've got three diversified stock indexes (US, international, and emerging markets), two government bond indices (US long-term Treasuries and global government bonds), a broad commodity index, plus gold and bitcoin. Each month – or every other month or every quarter, it’s your call – you’ll rank those eight diversified ETFs using momentum signals based on their average returns over the past three, six, nine, and 12 months. You’ll then:

-   Pick the top four that have the highest momentum.
-   Check each one’s absolute momentum. If it’s trending upward, you’ll keep it. If not, you’ll allocate that portion to a broad bond ETF.
-   Weight each selected asset equally (24% if bitcoin makes the cut, 25% if not).
-   Include bitcoin only when it ranks in the top four and shows positive absolute momentum, capping it at 4%. (You can also choose to skip bitcoin altogether if it’s not your jam.)
-   Rebalance monthly using the same process.

No economic views, no forecasts – just a disciplined, repeatable method. You can read about the rules in more detail here.

Why does it work?

This portfolio plays both offense and defense – and that’s key.

On offense, the strategy jumps on the bandwagon with what’s trending – capturing gains from strong-performing assets when markets are rising and avoiding assets that are going nowhere. That works because market trends often persist: economic cycles unfold gradually, and investors tend to pile into recent winners, reinforcing price moves. And this portfolio's concentrated positioning just helps amplify that effect.

On defense, it puts distance between itself and assets that are weakening, ducking into the safety of bonds when things turn. This helps it limit its drawdowns and protect its capital. That dual momentum approach – relative to pick leaders, absolute to cut losers – is what gives the portfolio resilience.

### What are the risks?

Like any trend-following strategy, the Momentum Rider has its flaws.

-   It can lag at major turning points when trends reverse quickly.
-   Range-bound markets can trigger whipsaw trades and small losses.
-   Sudden crashes can cause short-term pain before the next rebalance.
-   And yes, it probably will underperform in a wild stock rally – particularly when the S&P 500 or Nasdaq dominate.

The Momentum Rider’s off to a strong start. Since launching in November 2024, it’s gained 4.4% – comfortably ahead of global stocks (up 0.7%) and miles ahead of US stocks (down2%).

Look just at 2025, and it’s done even better: it’s up 4.4% year-to-date while global stocks have dipped slightly and US stocks are down 5%. That’s nearly ten percentage points of outperformance in just five months – from a strategy that’s designed to reduce risk.

## PART 3: The Finimize Sector Momentum Edge

The S&P 500’s recent success has mostly come down to one thing: tech dominance. But valuations are stretched and economic clouds have been forming, so a passive, tech-heavy investment like you’d get with an index-tracking ETF could leave you exposed if the tide turns. This strategy – the Sector Momentum Edge – offers a flexible, rules-based alternative way to play. It picks the strongest US stock sectors based on momentum and shuffles you into bondswhen no sector is in a clear uptrend. The goal is to piggyback on the winners, sidestep the rest, and cut your losses – all without trying to time the market or trade every single week.

### Who’s this strategy for (and who is it not for)?

The Sector Momentum Edge is for investors who aren’t interested in wild, whispered predictions about the next big thing – and would rather follow a rules-based system. It’s built for those who want a high probability of making steady returns across different market environments.

It’s also for people who want to stay activewithout managing their positions every day – and who care about avoiding big drawdowns, for both their financial and emotional peace of mind.

It’s not for day traders, nor is it for investors who can’t handle doing worse than tech stocks for a while – because yes, this strategy will sometimes shun the market’s darlings. It’s also not for purely passive investors: the strategy needs a few minutes of your time every month.

### What are the rules?

Each month, you’ll rank all 11 major US stock sectors using a momentum score based on their average returns over the past three, six, nine, and 12 months – skipping the most recent month to avoid the short-term mean-reversiondisruption that’s typically seen in stocks. You’ll then:

Pick the three top sectors by relative momentum.

Check their absolute momentum – if they’re trending upward, you’ll buy them; if not, you’ll shift that allocation into a bond ETF (like BNDor SUAG).

Invest equally across your three picks.

Rebalance monthly using the same rules.

That’s it. No complicated math, no gut feelings – just disciplined trend\-following.

### How has it performed historically?

Since 2000, the strategy has delivered annualized returns of 10%, beating the S&P 500’s 7%. So, if you’d started with ten grand, using the strategy back in Y2K, you’d now have over $105,000, compared to the $60,000 you’d have made investing in the S&P 500.

### Why does it work?

Like the Momentum Rider, this strategy isn’t interested in choosing between offense and defense – it plays the whole field.

When markets are flying, it grabs the wheel with both hands – riding leaders like energy during an inflation spiral or tech in growth booms. And thanks to human nature (think FOMO, herd mentality, recency bias) and economic cycles, trends usually stick around long enough to bank some solid gains.

But it’s not all gas pedal, no brakes. When the mood shifts, the strategy does too. It shifts into defensive sectors during mild downturns (like in 2022) and cozies up to bonds when everything’s falling (like in 2008). And that twin talent of using relative momentum to pursue the winners and absolute momentum to dodge the losers is what gives it its fierce edge.

### What are the risks?

Nothing nails the market 100% of the time.

-   It can be sluggish at turning points, clinging to yesterday’s heroes even after they start to stall.
-   In choppy, no-trend markets, it can have you entering and exiting trades at all the wrong times, leaving you with a stack of tiny losses.
-   And when sudden crashes happen – hello, 1987 – they can hit before the monthly rebalance kicks in.

That said, this strategy tends to hold its own – and then some. It’s a proven, data-driven, time-tested way to stick with what’s working – and ditch what’s not.

### How’s it performed since its inception?

Since launching in November, the Sector Momentum Edge has slipped 2.8% – admittedly, worse than the S&P’s 1.2% dropover the same period. But that’s not entirely unexpected. See, momentum strategies tend to struggle when markets get rocky and when leaders become laggards – and that’s exactly what’s been playing out lately. It’s a tradeoff you get with these strategies: they offer stronger protection in major downturns and can outperform across different environments, but they’re often more vulnerable at turning points.

Once you get through those periods, these strategies tend to find their feet. The Sector Momentum Edge, for example, has been doing better than the S&P 500 so far this year. It’s down 3.3% – compared to the index’s 4.4% slide.

Its positioning is well worth a look: while financials and communication stocks have been a staple of the strategy for over a year, it dumped tech last summer and warmed up to utilities instead. That shift showed the strategy was leaning more toward the defensive – and, honestly, it mostly paid off.

## Part 4: Here’s what to make of it all.

If you take one thing away from this, make it this: don’t bet your future on a single outcome. US stocks have been great – but so were Japanese stocks, commodities, and emerging markets back in their day.

Your first takeaway is this: if risk means not hitting your goals, then putting all your eggs in one basket is, well, risky. So make sure your portfolio can work in different environments – or better yet, can adapt to them.

Second, the process matters. A simple, rules-based strategy gives you discipline, helps you learn from what works (and what doesn’t), and keeps your emotions in check.
